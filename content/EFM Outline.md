#### A brief history of fracture mechanics :
- The industrial revolution in the 19th century led to increasing use of metals for structural applications
- Understanding their structural behaviour became ever important to design efficient engineering systems
- A mathematical framework was formed by testing various metals and structures but there were catastrophic failures, which clearly meant that there was a fundamental flaw or a gap in the way we understood structures 
- Locomotives that used boilers (pressure vessels) failed and the failures were traced out to [poor design choices, choice of material] and they were then improved by better design, materials and production methods
- People noted you cannot have a delinking of design and production method, which is where the modern idea of ==integrated design and manufacturing== comes from
- Despite these improvements there were failures, which still meant we didn't truly understand the behaviour under service loads
- These failures paved the way for 'Fracture Mechanics'

### What is fundamentally different in Fracture Mechanics ?
>Fracture Mechanics recognises the role of inherent flaws in structures and their affect on the performance and life during service

Note : In a graduate level course relating to solid mechanics, strength of materials, mechanics of solids etc. we have idealised the material to be homogenous and elastic continuum. But the reality is flaws exist in every material and they grow during service leading to failure. 

The application of fracture mechanics is the thus the development of what's called [Damage Tolerant Design Approach] - understanding that the design approach must account for the flaw and it's impact of service life and when the flaw becomes critical

- For this to happen we must understand the crack growth mechanism and material behaviour
- Fracture Mechanics is a confluence of :
	- Stress Analysis and Design
	- Material Science 
	- NDT (Non-Destructive Testing)
