Composite Overwrapped Pressure Vessel is a pressure vessel consisting of a thin liner wrapped around with structural fiber designed to hold liquid or gas under high pressure. The liner acts as a permeation barrier between the fluid and the composite overwrap to prevent leaks.

The liners can be made of rubber, plastic, or thin ductile metal. Their sole purpose is to maintain leakage and add little structural integrity. They have a very significant weight advantage over all metal vessels, but they have unique design, manufacturing, test and qualification requirements. The reason liners exists is because on its own, generally composite materials can be porous, so the permeation barrier to prevent fluids from escaping out is not good. 

Fiber are in a spool and they are fed into a resin bath, that wets the fibers and the fibers are then wrapped over the mandrel (in some cases this is the liner) at different ply angles. This process is also more commonly referred to as ['Filament Winding']. This is then placed in an Autoclave. Where it is heated and pressure cured to make the polymeric chains in the resin. 

The liner design in borrowed from the [[Pressure Vessel Design]]. But since the  interaction between composite overwrap and the liner is quite complex, different failure modes exist and are more complicated.
