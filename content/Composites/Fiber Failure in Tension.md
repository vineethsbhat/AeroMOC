- It is the final simultaneous fracture of many fiber bundles, due to the fact they are brittle behavior of fiber composites, the fiber fracture occurs suddenly without previous non-linearity. 
- No stress redistribution, it is the ultimate failure 
- This is the only desired way of failure because the fibers are the ones carrying load and you should the structure in such way you dimension it for this failure mode. ([So the “best case” is: everything else (matrix + interface) is strong enough that the fibers get fully used.])

>If something else fails first, you’re leaving fiber strength on the table. If the matrix cracks, or the interface debonds, or delamination starts _before_ the fibers reach their tensile strength, the laminate’s stiffness and load-sharing collapses early. That’s basically “we paid for high-strength fibers but didn’t get to use them.”

>One nuance: in real structures, you often _like_ matrix cracking / delamination to be detectable and progressive (warning) _before_ ultimate failure. But you still want the **final** strength ceiling to be **fiber tension**, not interface/matrix.

Two modes are :
1. Failure of the fiber itself : A fiber reaches its tensile strength and snaps. That’s the “clean” fiber mode.
2. Fiber/Matrix Interface Failure : At first it sounds like “that’s not fiber failure, that’s interface failure.” A fiber embedded in resin doesn’t magically carry load by itself. The matrix “grabs” the fiber and transfers load into it through [interfacial shear] (and radial/hoop stresses near the interface). [In any reinforcement based failure, bond failure is always treated as a reinforcement related failure mode, e.g., Rebar in concrete] 

>Real Failure is not purely one mechanism, it combines both. Few fibers break first then, that causes local stress concentration and then it leads to local debond, fibers will get pulled out and eventually final fracture.  FF in tension typically occurs around 70-80 % of final failure load.
