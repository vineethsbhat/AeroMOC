In CLT Analysis we saw we have certain inputs and outputs :
a. Input : Laminate loads (forces and moments)
b. O/P : Results (stresses and strain in all plies)

Failure analysis is a key part of designing structure to understand real world behaviour and they help us dimension our component. 

>[But first, what does Failure Even Mean ?]

We know composites are heterogenous materials where complex failure modes can appear (can fail in many different ways)
The 3 main ways are :
1. Fiber Failure 
2. Matrix Cracking 
3. Delamination : [Decohesion Elements are used to understand the propagation of delamination]

>[!Important ]
>At the micromechanical level one can say failure occurs when it is of fiber and matrix or failure between fiber and matrix interface. Since it is heterogenous in nature and you have several layers, the stresses in the laminates vary layer wise depending on ply angle or ply material, so there's no simultaneous failure of all plies. The ply with the highest ratio of applied stress to strength will fail

First Ply Failure : The first point in the laminate where the laminate fails
Last Ply Failure : Point of failure that leads to total laminate failure
