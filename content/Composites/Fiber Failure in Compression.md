Fiber Failure in compression can be caused by fiber fracture, micro buckling of the fibers or fiber kinking. ==It is important to remember; In compression, a fiber is like a very slender column: it wants to **buckle/kink** long before you “crush” the material. compressive strength is rarely limited by the intrinsic compressive strength of carbon fibers. It’s usually limited by a stability failure of the fiber network, enabled by the matrix==

>[In a fiber bundle, the fibers are not perfectly aligned to carry load, this initial misalignment causes shear stresses which makes the fibers bend and rotate, this local shear induced deformation is called ==fiber kinking== which happens along the band of the matrix]

A few ways this occurs :
1. The waviness in the fibers under compressive load have a tendency to rotate the fibers and when the matrix resists this rotation, there is a small region where (band) the fibers deflect sideways in the microscale. (Micro-buckling)
2. A rather famous failure mode is 'Kink Banding', [“The fibers start to rotate/deflect due to misalignment; the matrix resists via shear. Once that shear resistance softens (elastic limit / yielding / damage), the deformation localizes into a kink band.”]

>[!Important]
>Why this is undesirable ?
>1. very defect sensitive (waviness, voids, resin rich pockets etc.)
>2. matrix behaviour is based on its shear behaviour 
>3. less predictable 
>4. can be because of manufacturing issues

[Note :] Fiber failure in compression is also a coupled failure mode between fiber stability + matrix shear failure.
