In IFF in compression, the fracture plane is not equal to action plane of loading. Transverse compression stresses cannot cause failure in action plane of stresses. This means the cracks form on a kind of diagonal or a different plane than the direction compression load and this because of the way the material shears and buckles under compressive load.

>The idea of a fracture plane occurring at an angle to the action plane is not limited to composites, it is a general material response to compressive load.

A few key things to know :
1. Transverse compressive failure occurs on a plane inclined by $50 \degree$ to the action plane, therefore it is a shear induced failure mode. 
2. Compressive failure (tension or compression) leads to wedge fracture of the lamina. As the cracks grow along the length of the fiber, the separation between the matrix induces a force in the thickness direction leading to what we call as [[Delamination]]
3. This results in total failure of the laminate 

This form of IFF ([Delamination]) is regarded as one of the main final failure mode of laminate because the layers of laminate are no longer working together to support and share the load. And the entire structure becomes compromised, in any case, a mode where the entire structure becomes compromised is a final failure mode.

The most commonly affected structures in delamination are 
1. Torsional Springs
2. Pressure Vessels External Pressure (submerged vessels)

> One way to reduce this effect is by using thin plies at various angles. If you fibers in the loading direction then the strain level is reduced.
