The key challenge in designing aerospace structures comes down to optimising 3 things :
- Cost
- Performance
- Safety

As a structural design and analysis engineer there are a few things we must always keep in mind

- Strength vs. Weight : The "Golden Rule" of aerospace. A structure must be strong enough to survive launch or flight loads but light enough to actually get off the ground.
- Complex Loads : You have complex combined loading conditions 
- Material Selection : The choice of material influences structural behaviour
- Damage Tolerance + Preventive Maintenance 
- Manufacturing Constraints 
- Regulatory and Certification Requirements 

There's a fundamental difference in the operation of rockets and aircrafts. The operational environment plays a crucial role in the design process. We also look at the kinds of primary loads that act on the system, the reusability and the material usage. 

It is important to note the design rules are not hard and fast, and are widely applicable to systems in both aerospace and aviation but despite this there is a difference in the approach.


>Aircrafts need to have modular design, they have to be inspected frequently, they are safety critical and have a higher FOS, they are operated frequently and thus are prone to high cycle fatigue. 

A launch vehicle is subjected to different kinds of loads :
1. High Axial Loads
2. Dynamic Pressure ($Q_{max}$)
3. Vibration and Shock
4. Acoustic 
5. Thermal gradients 
And they operate at extreme temperatures albeit for a short duration. 

| **Feature**      | **Aircraft**                           | **Rockets**                                |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Primary Load** | Aerodynamic lift and maneuver loads.   | Axial thrust and massive vibrations.       |
| **Service Life** | Designed for 20-30 years of daily use. | Often designed for a single use (minutes). |
| **Pressure**     | Constant cycling (cabin pressure).     | Extreme internal pressure (fuel tanks).    |
