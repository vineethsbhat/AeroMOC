When designing a structure several input quantities are not known. And these are :
1. Applied loads : Limit load and Ultimate load and a certain degree of conservatism 
2. Variability in Usage : Different load types, load paths, depending on what aspect of flight you are looking at
3. Material Scatter : Material variability is inherent to structures and materials, design must account for this variation (A-Basis and B-Basis allowables)

A Basis value is used for single load path primary structure where failure leads to loss of structural integrity and B Basis is used for structures with multiple load path and secondary structures

Environmental Considerations and RTA (Room Temperature Ambient) Properties :

- Increasing temperature and moisture content both reduce the strength of the material.
- Temperature tests : CTA, RTA, ETA

Effect of Damage :

Composites have a very high sensitivity to notches.  A notch can be any form of damage, such as impact, cack or a cut-out

Of the most critical : Impact damage is the most important. The way damage analysis is done is largely dependent on method of inspection 

If the threshold of detectability is lower than the inspection method, then it must be capable of withstanding ultimate load without failure. In practice most common inspection method used is visual inspection. 

More detailed reliable inspection (ultrasound, x-ray etc.) are done at the depot level 
There are two kinds of damage :
1. BVID : must withstand UL. 1mm deep indentation 
2. VID : must withstand UL. Must be visible from a distance of 1.5 m in ambient light
3. NVID : detailed inspection needed to determine UL or LL limit 

Safe Return to Base Load : 80 % of LL to make sure no failure even in presence of larger damage like bird strike, or lightening strike

Note : ==The structure is always designed for the worst case of damage/flaw since this covers other cases==

>Knockdown factor accounts for both the worst environment of operation as well as worst type of damage and material strength. The statistical values that come out of performing the strength tests are what's called 'Design Allowable'

For failure, in terms of design we talk about loading index (also more commonly called as stress exposure), reserve factor or FOS
