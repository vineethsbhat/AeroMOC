1. Material is considered to be homogenous and an elastic continuum
2. Knowledge gained in this first level course in limited due to the vast number of assumptions made
3. We start with a simple tension test on a simple specimen in a UTM machine. We plot the load vs. displacement curve and then convert it to stress vs. strain curve. We have an initial linear region, followed by a yield region and then work hardening, and then UTS and finally fracture where the material separates.
4. Elastic region and plastic regions are understood. Thus a ductile material gives us a warning before failure, but in reality this doesn't happen
5. In case of the spectacular failures ductile materials failed in brittle fashion, so the question is what aspect of the behaviour was not included in the conventional analysis we performed. That was a very simple test enough to isolate and characterise isotropic material behaviour

---
1. Now we moved onto the idea of bending. We derived a mathematical model from [Euler-Bernoulli Beam Theory] and came up with the flexural formula
$$\frac{M_b}{I_z} = \frac{\sigma_x}{y} = \frac{E}{\rho}$$
Note : we made some assumptions, plane sections remain plane before and after loading
2. Understanding that the stresses are highest at the top and bottom layers and min. at the middle (does not contribute to load carrying capacity) layers allowed us to design efficient structures (cross section of railway lines)
3. Despite this, the rails fractured in service due to repeated loading and inherent flaws

---
1. Then we looked at twisting of a shaft in torsion. We assumed radial lines remain straight and radial and we got the flexural formula oof torsion
$$\frac{M_t}{I_p} = \frac{\tau_{\theta}}{r} = \frac{G \phi}{L}$$
2. We saw shear stress varies linearly and is max. at the surface and 0 at the center. 

So all of this helped us understand Young's modulus and possion's ratio and this allowed us to characterise the material. We now know how a truss is efficient in material usage and how a hollow shaft is preferred or why a railway lines takes a certain shape.

---
1. We also realised that in real life you have a combination of tension, bending, torsion and we developed failure theories based on the shape change (yield theories). 
2. We developed the concept of principle stresses this allowed us to remove the dependence on co-ordinate system and understand the combined effect to some extent. [Take a look at the idea of Principle Stress and Strain] and how it's related to [[Von-Misses Failure Theory]]
3. You have many theories, so there's no way to predict what really happens to the material.
